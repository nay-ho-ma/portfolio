Contracts AI

Helping labels and business managers understand, verify, and trust their contracts — without a law degree.

Context

Tone is a royalty management platform for independent music labels and distributors. At the heart of what Tone does is contracts — the agreements that determine how every dollar of revenue gets split between a label and its artists. Before a label can process a single royalty period, every contract has to be entered into the system accurately. That's a harder job than it sounds.

The people responsible for this aren't lawyers. They're label managers, business managers, and operations teams who are handed dense legal documents and expected to interpret them correctly — with real financial consequences if they get it wrong.

Contract UI overview — before state

The problem

Contract setup is the biggest blocker in the royalty processing workflow. Users had to read and interpret every contract themselves, translate legal language into system configuration, and hope they got it right. There was no way to verify their work short of running a full royalty calculation and seeing what came out. For new customers especially, this was overwhelming — and a poor early experience meant we risked losing them before they ever saw the value of the platform.

PDF reader feature
Contract analyzer

My contribution

There was no brief for this project. Tone had a company goal to incorporate AI as a competitive advantage, with real pressure to be first-to-market. I collaborated with engineering leads to identify where to start — they scoped what was technically feasible, and I advocated for the workflows where users felt the most pain.

Contracts rose to the top because it's a pain point for every single customer, and it hits hardest during onboarding — the moment when a new customer is deciding whether Tone was the right choice. A win here wasn't just a feature, it was a retention play.

I designed three interconnected features: a PDF reader that extracts and surfaces key contract terms; a contract analyzer that compares a user's configuration against their uploaded PDF to catch setup errors; and a chat interface that lets users ask questions and simulate royalty calculations before committing to a real period.

AI chat interface

The key decision

When the MVP was ready, there was pressure from leadership to ship. I pushed back. Our testing had shown that the AI responses were too long, inconsistent, and not quite useful enough. Shipping something that didn't actually help users — especially at the moment they needed confidence most — felt like it would do more damage than waiting. I advocated for refining our prompts before launch, and we did.

When we did ship, we kept it quiet — beta badges, organic discovery, no announcement. That gave us time to iterate with real feedback before a wider release coordinated with product marketing.

Beta badge treatment
Product marketing landing

Outcome

Contracts AI has become a selling point in product demos and a recurring highlight in onboarding training calls. Prospects ask about it. New customers respond to it. It's one of the clearest signals that Tone is investing in making a genuinely hard job easier — not just for power users, but for anyone who's ever been handed a contract and told to figure it out.

Homepage Redesign

Turning a page everyone skipped in demos into a personalized launchpad that adapts to where you are in your journey.

Context

Royalty processing is a multi-step, high-stakes workflow. There's a lot to manage — sales data, costs, tracks, releases, contracts, payments, and payees — and knowing what to do at any given moment, especially when you're new, can be genuinely overwhelming. The homepage should be the place that orients you. For about a year and a half, ours wasn't doing that job.

Original homepage — before state

The problem

The original homepage was a holdover from the first version of the app — a duplicate of the main navigation that added no new information and reflected none of the workflows we had built since launch. In demos, we skipped it. Users learned to ignore it. I took it upon myself to fix it.

My contribution

The core challenge: the same page needed to serve two meaningfully different users — not different people, but the same person at different points in their journey. A new customer needs orientation. An existing customer needs a status check.

I tackled this in three phases. First, a dedicated onboarding checklist that graduated users to a full homepage. Then a stopgap letting onboarding users skip ahead after hearing they wanted the full view earlier. Finally, a unified adaptive experience — onboarding tasks became default to-do items, and completing them unlocked progressive ones.

Phase 1 — onboarding checklist
Phase 3 — unified to-do
Full homepage — final state

Built with Claude Code

This was the first project where I used Claude Code to implement my own designs end-to-end — one backend PR for a new API, mini app, and database migration; one frontend PR for all UI changes. Worked in parallel on a merged branch, tested locally, pushed to staging.

The key decision

The pivot to a single unified experience wasn't in the original plan — it came from listening. A customer's reaction during an onboarding call made it clear the checklist and the to-do list were solving the same underlying need. Merging them was the more honest reflection of how users actually think about their work.

Outcome

The homepage went from a page everyone skipped in demos to one that customers respond to immediately. Our head of customer support saw it happen in real time — a new customer lit up when they saw the to-do list. That reaction directly shaped what the feature became.

Unmapped Sales

Turning a growing pile of unresolved royalties into an actionable, in-app workflow — so artists actually get paid.

Context

When a label uploads a sales file into Tone, each row represents a single sale — and there can be hundreds of thousands of them. Every row needs to connect to a track or release, which then connects to a contract, so Tone knows how to calculate and pay out royalties. When a row can't be matched, it becomes an unmapped sale.

Unmapped sales are money in limbo. For artists, it's royalties they're owed but haven't received. For labels, it's a financial liability they have to track and account for over time. And it's a problem that exists at every music company.

Unmapped sales overview — before state

The problem

Before this tool existed, resolving unmapped sales was a manual, offline process. Download a report, parse the data yourself, manually create tracks and releases, re-upload, re-run. Tedious, easy to deprioritize, and the pile just kept growing — period after period.

My contribution

I designed an in-app tool that replaced the entire offline workflow. Users can now view, sort, and filter unmapped sales directly in Tone. When a row includes a product identifier like an ISRC or UPC, users can search Spotify for metadata and import it directly. When new products are saved, the sales file reprocesses automatically.

Table sorting — revenue view
Table sorting — artist view
Spotify metadata import flow

The key decision

User research revealed two distinct mental models: a revenue POV and an artist POV. The solution was a flexible table with sorting and filtering — supported by collapsible rows to reveal individual sales without overwhelming the view.

The Spotify metadata integration turned a lookup-and-type workflow into an import — one decision that dramatically reduced the friction of resolving a row when you're staring down thousands of them.

Outcome

Internal users say this tool has completely changed their workflow. The process that used to get punted every period is now something people can actually make progress on. No other royalty platform has tools like this. For independent labels trying to make sure their artists get paid, that gap matters.
