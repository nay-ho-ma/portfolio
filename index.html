A little about me

I'm naturally curious, always following threads, always wondering what else is possible, and I do my best work on teams where that's encouraged. That's how I've ended up spanning strategy, research, systems thinking, and increasingly, the codebase itself — contributing code, learning how our frontend libraries work, and considering the sequence of what needs to be built when I'm designing something new.

What that's given me practically: I now catch things I used to miss. When should a page auto-refresh? When does an error message show? When does a user need to hit save, and when should it just happen? These patterns are difficult to specify in Figma and easy to get wrong. Being closer to the code means I think about them earlier, and more consistently across the product.

I'm also just genuinely interested in the people I work with. I'll notice a new haircut on a video call, ask to see your cat if it wanders into the background, and always want to hear about your weekend. Especially on remote teams, that kind of easy back-and-forth can sometimes feel forced, but I actually care.

How I work

I'm most energized by the creative process in full — understanding the problem, iterating, prototyping, building, shipping. AI has meaningfully accelerated that loop for me: I can test ideas faster, get closer to real implementations, and spend more time on the decisions that actually matter.

  • I own problems end-to-end, from ambiguous user feedback to shipped product
  • I stay a few steps ahead and keep stakeholders informed without being asked
  • I push back when something doesn't serve a real user need (and I explain why)
  • I collaborate directly with engineering, including contributing code
  • I design with implementation in mind, always considering sequence, constraints, and what's actually buildable
